# Acid jazz

Touchpoints: late-80s–90s UK acid jazz (Brand New Heavies, Incognito, Galliano, Corduroy, early Jamiroquai, Young Disciples), plus its roots in 70s jazz-funk and soul-jazz. Stylistic references only.

**Tempo**: 95–120 BPM, funk/soul grooves with live-band feel.

## Harmonic palette

- **Dominant vamps**: 9 and 13 chords held for bars (`D9`, `G13`), mixolydian funk.
- **Minor 7/9 vamps** (dorian): `Em9` → `A9` (i–IV dorian: the IV is major — that's the soul).
- ii–V movement, often with chromatic planing of 9th chords (`Eb9 E9 F9` slides).
- Gospel/soul moves: IV/I, backdoor iv–bVII, `Ab/Bb`-type slash dominants, passing diminished chords.
- #9 and 13 on dominants; sus chords for horns.

## Progression shapes

- `Em9 A9` — dorian i–IV vamp.
- `Cm9 F13` — the same idea in C minor.
- `Dm9 G13 Cmaj9 Fmaj9` — ii–V–I–IV soul-jazz loop.
- `Fm9 Bb13 Ebmaj9 Abmaj9 Dm7b5 G7#9 Cm9` — longer jazz-funk chain.
- `E9 F9 F#9 G9` — chromatic 9th-chord planing (horn/organ hits).

## Voicing and instruments

Rhodes/Wurlitzer and Hammond organ (quartal, close), clavinet (short, funky 2–3 note shapes), horn sections in 3–4-part close harmony (guide tones + 9/13), wah guitar playing 9th-chord shapes in the upper register.

## Bass relationship

Funk bass: 16th syncopation, octaves, ghost notes, slides; root on beat 1, movement elsewhere; chromatic approach into chord changes. Bass and drums lock; chords comp around them.

## Rhythm and melody

Syncopated comping, anticipations (the push), 16th swing. Horn lines and vocal hooks: blues/pentatonic with chromatic approach notes; call-and-response between horns and vocal/keys.

## Arrangement

Song forms (verse/chorus/bridge), solos over the vamp, breakdown to bass+drums with keys stabs, horn-section hits on transitions.

## Pitfalls

Making it too smooth (keep the funk in the bass and rhythm); full-band density in every bar — leave space for the bass.
