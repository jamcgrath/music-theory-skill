# Jazzy / liquid / atmospheric drum and bass

Lineage: mid-to-late-90s atmospheric/"intelligent" DnB (the Good Looking Records sound — LTJ Bukem, Blame, Seba, PFM, Big Bud), jazz-step and Reinforced-era jazz influences, then 2000s liquid funk (Calibre, Marcus Intalex, High Contrast, Hospital-style soul) and today's soulful liquid. Artist names are stylistic touchpoints only — write original material.

**Tempo**: 170–176 BPM today; late-90s atmospheric often 160–170. Harmony and melody are felt at half-time (85–88).

## Harmonic palette

- Minor 9 and minor 11 chords as home (dorian colour: the raised 6th shows up as the 13 or as a major IV).
- maj7, maj9 and **maj7#11** (lydian) for lift and "sunrise" moments.
- 9sus4 / 13sus4 dominants instead of hard V7s; occasional 7#9/7b9 as a single moment of tension.
- **Planing** (constant structure): one m9 or maj9 shape moved by whole tones or minor thirds — the key atmospheric 90s move.
- Pedal points: the sub holds the root while pads drift through 2–3 chords.
- Two-chord oscillations that never "resolve" are normal; the drums provide motion.

## Progression shapes (examples, transpose freely)

- `Fm9 Bbm9` — i9–iv9 vamp, 2–4 bars each (aeolian float; make it `Bb13` for the dorian version).
- `Fm9 Ebm9 Dbm9 Ebm9` — one m9 shape planed down by whole tones and back up (constant-structure drift).
- `Ebmaj9 Fm9 Gm9 Fm9` — diatonic steps in Eb; liquid-style warmth.
- `Dbmaj7#11 Cm9 Bbm9 Abmaj9` — descending stepwise roots, lydian top.
- `Am9 D13 Gmaj9 Cmaj7#11` — soulful ii–V–I–IV (2000s liquid).
- `Cm11 Abmaj7#11 Fm9 G7#9` — minor loop with a single tense turnaround chord.

## Voicing and instruments

- Rhodes (rootless 4-note, sustained or gently comped), long-release pads (open voicings, top note held across changes), occasional jazz guitar or vibes.
- Strings/pads move slowly: favour common-tone voice leading so pads glide; `--top-line` on a held note.
- 90s atmospheric: lush pads and sampled jazz chords (often a single sampled chord pitched to new roots — i.e. planing). Recreate with `stab`/`pad` and identical shapes.

## Bass relationship

- Sub plays long root notes (1–2 bars), sometimes gliding into the next root; a mid-bass layer may add a simple 2-step figure.
- Avoid inversions in the sub (the 3rd in the sub can sound like a different key at club volume); put inversions in the keys' bass voice instead.
- Bass notes on 1 and the "and" of 3 (with the two-step kick), or one long note per chord. Leave the snare (beats 2 and 4) clean.

## Rhythm and melody

- Chord changes every 1, 2 or 4 bars; stabs/comps are sparse and often anticipate the bar by an 8th.
- Melodies: long notes, pentatonic/dorian, 9ths and 11ths as resting points; vocal chops and flute/sax-like lines; call-and-response across 4-bar phrases.

## Arrangement

32-bar beatless or filtered intro with pads and a pedal; drop at 32 or 64; breakdown that strips to pads/Rhodes with a reharmonised or inverted version of the main loop; second drop with a variation (new turnaround, counter-melody).

## Pitfalls

Over-dense 6-note voicings fighting the reese; hard functional V7–i cadences every 4 bars (sounds like pop-jazz, not liquid); chords in the low-mid that mask the bass.
