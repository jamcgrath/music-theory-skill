# Arrangement (composition across sections)

Focus: how harmony, melody and bass change across a track. Not mixing or sound design.

## Section roles and harmony

| Section | Harmonic job | Typical moves |
|---|---|---|
| Intro | establish mood, withhold information | pedal bass or no bass; one chord or a 2-chord fragment; pads/filtered keys; the progression's colour without its motion |
| Build | increase expectation | add the chord changes; shorten harmonic rhythm; dominant pedal; rising top voice |
| Main / drop | full statement | full progression, bass on roots, hook/melody |
| Breakdown | contrast, emotion | strip to pads/keys; reharmonise (borrowed chords, inversions); slower harmonic rhythm; let the melody be exposed |
| Second drop | familiarity + development | same progression with a variation (new last chord, new bass inversion, counter-melody) |
| Outro | release | return to the intro material; DJ-friendly in club genres |

## Phrase lengths

Club genres work in 8/16/32-bar phrases; changes land on phrase boundaries. DnB intros/outros of 16–32 bars with sparse harmony help DJs mix. Downtempo and acid jazz can be freer (verse/chorus forms, 12-bar blues forms, 4+4 vamps).

## Repetition without monotony

Change one element per 4 or 8 bars, keeping the rest:
- **Voicing** (same chords, top voice moves to a new note — `--top-line`)
- **Rhythm** (sustained → stabs → anticipated)
- **Bass** (root → inversions/approach notes on the second pass)
- **Colour** (m7 → m9 → m11 as the track builds)
- **Turnaround** (bar 4 of every 4th repetition becomes a tritone sub, secondary dominant or chromatic approach)
- **Register** (the same progression an octave up on a different instrument)
- **Subtraction** (drop the 3rds for a section, or the bass, then bring them back)

## Transitions

- Pickup chord on the last beat of a phrase (chromatic approach or V7 of the new section's first chord).
- Dominant pedal (hold the V in the bass for 2–4 bars before the drop).
- Harmonic silence: drop all harmony for a bar; the return lands harder.
- Modulation for a lift: up a whole step or minor third at a section boundary, prepared by its V7 — use rarely.

## Tension curve

Plan tension at the track level: intro low (static, modal), build rising (more dominants, faster changes), drop resolved but energetic, breakdown emotional (borrowed chords, exposed melody), final section the richest version. Write it as a one-line map per section before writing notes when the user asks for a full arrangement.

## Genre lengths (rough)

DnB 5–7 min (intro 32 bars, two drops); house 6–8 min (long intros/outros); downtempo 4–6 min; acid jazz 4–6 min (song form). See genre files for specifics.
