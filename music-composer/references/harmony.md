## 1. Chord anatomy

- **Guide tones (3rd and 7th) define the chord.** Major/minor comes from the 3rd; maj7/dominant/minor-7 from the 7th. Keep them in almost every voicing.
- **The root** is usually played by the bass. Keys can leave it out (rootless voicing) — cleaner, less mud.
- **The 5th** is filler unless altered (b5, #5). First note to drop.
- **Extensions (9, 11, 13)** add colour. The highest named one is the point of the chord (keep it); lower ones are optional.
- Natural 11 over a major 3rd (maj7, dominant) is a minor 9th clash — omit it or use #11. That's why `C13` contains no F.

## 2. Extensions: what fits where

| Chord | Safe colours | Spicier | Avoid / handle with care |
|---|---|---|---|
| maj7 | 9, 13 (6) | #11 (lydian float) | natural 11 |
| m7 | 9, 11 | 13 (dorian, soulful) | b9 |
| dominant 7 | 9, 13 | b9, #9, #11, b13, alt | natural 11 (use sus4 instead) |
| m7b5 (ø) | 11, b13 | natural 9 | b9 in close voicings |
| dim7 | any note a whole step above a chord tone | | |
| sus4 / 7sus4 | 9, 13 | b9 (phrygian sus) | the major 3rd in the same register |

Rule of thumb: an extension that belongs to the scale the chord is "borrowing" sounds intentional; one that doesn't sounds like a wrong note unless it moves somewhere by step.

## 3. Functional harmony

Three jobs: **tonic** (home: I, vi, iii / i, bIII), **pre-dominant** (moving away: ii, IV / iiø, iv, bVI), **dominant** (wants home: V7, viiø).

- The dominant's pull comes from the tritone between its 3rd and 7th (G7: B and F) collapsing by step into the tonic's root and 3rd (C and E). Explain resolution as "these two notes squeeze inward".
- **ii–V–I** is the core jazz move: Dm9 – G13 – Cmaj9. In minor: Gø7 – C7alt – Fm9.
- Root motion down a 5th (= up a 4th) is the strongest "this leads somewhere" motion; motion by step or third is softer; chromatic slides are smooth and modern.
- Loops: in a 4-bar loop the last chord leads into the first. A V-type or chromatic-approach chord in bar 4 makes the loop restart with a sense of arrival.

## 4. Nonfunctional and modal harmony

Much electronic music doesn't resolve — it *hovers*. Tools:

- **Modal vamps.** Two chords from one mode, neither acting as a dominant: dorian `i9 – IV9` (Dm9–G9 or Dm9–G13), aeolian `i – bVII`, lydian `Imaj7 – II` or `II7` over the same bass (Fmaj7 – G/F or G7/F; the B natural is the lydian note), mixolydian `I7 – bVII` (funk).
- **Constant structure / planing.** Keep one chord shape and move it to non-diatonic roots (Fm9 → Ebm9 → Dbm9 → Em9). The ear follows the colour, not a key. Signature of deep house "chord memory" stabs and 90s atmospheric DnB pads.
- **Pedal point.** Hold one bass note while chords change above it (Fm9, Gbmaj7/F, Ebm9/F). Builds tension or a trance-like float; great for intros and breakdowns.
- **Chromatic mediants.** Major chords a third apart sharing only one or two notes (Cmaj7 – Abmaj7, Cmaj7 – Emaj7). Cinematic, bright-shift; use once, not every bar.
- **Parallel motion of the whole voicing** is fine and idiomatic here — it's a sound, not an error.

## 5. Progression devices

| Device | Example | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| ii–V–I | Dm9 G13 Cmaj9 | classic jazz arrival |
| minor ii–V–i | Gm7b5 C7alt Fm9 | darker, more tension |
| Backdoor | Fm7 Bb7 Cmaj7 (iv–bVII–I) | soft, soulful arrival without V |
| Circle-of-fifths chain | Em7 A7 Dm7 G7 Cmaj7 | momentum, "falling forward" |
| Descending bass / line cliché | Am Am(maj7) Am7 Am6 | moving inner line under a static chord |
| Deceptive cadence | G7 → Am7 (instead of C) | sets up an expectation and swerves |
| Turnaround | Cmaj7 A7 Dm7 G7 | gets the loop back to bar 1 |
| Two-chord oscillation | Fm9 ↔ Bbm9 (or Ebm9/F) | hypnotic, loop-native |
| Chromatic approach | Abmaj9 → A7 → Bbm9 | slides into the target from a semitone away |

Four-chord loop shapes that work across these genres (in F minor / Ab major): `Fm9 Bbm9 Eb13 Abmaj9` (aeolian — or vi–ii–V–I of Ab: the relative-major drift), `Fm9 Dbmaj9 Ebm9 Ab13` (iii–I–ii–V of Db: a major-key loop that starts on its minor chord), `Fm9 Gbmaj7#11 Ebm9 Db69` (phrygian colour: the bII Gbmaj7 darkens it before settling toward Db), `Bbm9 Eb13 Abmaj9 Dbmaj9` (ii–V–I–IV, soul-jazz).

## 6. Sus, quartal, slash chords, upper structures

- **Sus chords** replace the 3rd with the 4th (or 2nd). `C9sus4` (= Gm7/C; `Bbmaj7/C` gives C13sus4) is the "smooth jazz/neo-soul dominant": tension without urgency.
- **Quartal voicings** stack 4ths (D G C F, or E A D G B — the "So What" voicing over E = Em11). Open, modern, ambiguous; great on Rhodes and pads.
- **Slash chords**: an upper chord over a different bass note. Name what the user hears; explain as "this chord with that bass".
  - `Ab/Bb` (= Bb9sus4/Bb11): gospel/house dominant.
  - `Ebmaj7/F` (= F13sus4): floating dominant.
  - `Abmaj7/F` (= Fm9) vs `Abmaj7`: same upper notes; the bass decides which you hear.
- **Upper-structure triads over dominants** (triad on top, guide tones below): over C7 — `D` triad → 9 #11 13 (C13#11); `Ab` triad → b13 1 #9 (C7#9b13); `Eb` triad → #9 5 b7; `Gb` triad → b5 b7 b9 (C7b9#11). Verify any you use with `mc.py check`.

## 7. Ambiguity

The same notes can be two chords (Ab C Eb G = Abmaj7, or Fm9 without its root; C E G A = C6 or Am7/C). When notes are ambiguous, present the plausible readings and say what tips it (bass note, what comes next, which chord the loop rests on). `mc.py identify` ranks readings and flags rootless and inverted interpretations.
